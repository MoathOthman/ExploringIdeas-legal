Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

The short version: GymLedger has no account and no servers for your data. Every workout you log — exercises, sets, reps, weights, routines, and bodyweight — is stored entirely on your device and never transmitted to us. There is no login and no cloud sync. What does leave your phone: anonymous usage statistics and an anonymous device record; if you choose to use the "Suggest a Feature" form, what you type in it together with the email address that form asks for, so we can reply; if you allow notifications, an anonymous push token, which also goes to Apple's and Google's notification delivery services; and the purchase status check described under Purchases. Each is described in full below.

Your workout data stays on your device

GymLedger is a local-first workout logger. All of your training data — exercises, sets, reps, weights, routines, workout history, and bodyweight entries — is stored on your iPhone using Apple's SwiftData framework. This data never leaves your device and is never transmitted to us or anyone else. There is no GymLedger account, no sign-up, no login, and no cloud sync in this version of the app.

Anonymous analytics — the honest disclosure

Your training data never leaves your device. Three things in GymLedger send something to services we run; the purchase status check, which goes to RevenueCat, is described under Purchases:

  • "Suggest a Feature" form — if you tap it and submit a suggestion, we receive the text you typed and the email address the form asks for. The email is required to send the form, because a suggestion we cannot reply to helps neither of us. Nothing is sent unless you press Send, and the form is entirely optional — you never have to open it.
  • In-app cross-promotion of our other apps — when the app shows you one of our other apps, it records that this happened so we can understand which promotions are useful.
  • Our own notification service — described in the next section. It is the one that sends something without you doing anything.

Each of these carries a random, anonymous install identifier (a random value generated on first launch that is not your Apple ID, your name, or any account), plus the app's version and the type of event. A feature suggestion also carries the text you typed, the email address you entered, the category you picked, the price you said you would pay (only if you entered one), whether a purchase is active, and your device model, iOS version and language/region. It is stored in a Google Cloud Firestore database that we control, used only to answer you and to decide what to build. Please do not put sensitive information in it; to have a record deleted, email us at the address under Contact.

The feedback form is the one place where what we hold is linked to you, because an email address can identify you. Everything else on this page is anonymous.

Our own notification service, and notifications

What: a device record held by our own service (not a third party), containing a random app-scoped device identifier, the platform, your iOS version, the app version, your language and time zone, whether you have allowed notifications, and whether app announcements are on. Alongside it we record that a session started, that you opened a notification, and, when the app shows you an in-app announcement, that it was shown, tapped or closed, with that announcement's identifier.

When: on launch, whether or not you allow notifications. It is not tied to notification permission, and we say so rather than implying the app is silent until you opt in. The record never contains your workouts, sets, reps, weights, routines or bodyweight.

Why: to deliver announcements, to count how many devices use the app, and to choose which devices receive one — which is marketing, and we say so. Push announcements are opt-in: you receive announcement notifications only if app announcements are turned on. Separately, the app may occasionally show an announcement on screen while you are using it (for example about a new feature or one of our other apps); that switch does not control those, and you can close one with a tap. The screen that asks for notification permission says that allowing notifications also turns app announcements on; you can switch them off at any time in GymLedger under Settings → Notifications → App announcements, and workout reminders are a separate switch that stays off until you turn it on.

The push token: if you allow notifications, your device generates an anonymous push registration token (a random identifier, not your name, email or Apple ID) which is added to that record so a notification can be delivered. Delivery itself goes through Apple's push service and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, so once you allow notifications Google receives the push token and a random Firebase installation identifier. We use Firebase for message delivery only; no Google analytics or advertising SDK is included. If you never allow notifications, no token is ever created — but the device record above is still sent. The message text is written by us and carries no personal data.

Not linked to you, not tracking: the device record carries no name, email or account, so we cannot tell who you are from it. It is our own first-party service: we do not sell it, share it with data brokers or ad networks, or combine it with data from other companies' apps or websites. Opting out: turn off app announcements to stop announcement notifications, or turn off notifications for GymLedger in iOS Settings to stop all notifications.

What GymLedger does not do

To be explicit:

  • We do not transmit your workouts, sets, reps, weights, routines, or bodyweight to any server — not in analytics, not in a notification, and not in the suggestion form.
  • We do not use the advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • We do not track you across apps, websites, or companies, and we do not use Apple's App Tracking Transparency framework because there is no cross-app tracking.
  • We show no ads and contain no third-party advertising SDK.
  • We do not access your contacts, calendar, photos, or location.
  • We do not send any Apple Health data off your device (see below).
  • We do not sell or share your data — there is nothing personal to sell.

Apple Health (HealthKit)

Apple Health is optional and off until you turn it on. If you connect it, iOS asks for your permission first, and GymLedger then does two things: it saves each finished workout to Apple Health as a strength-training workout (start time, end time and duration), so it appears in Fitness, and it reads your bodyweight from Apple Health so the app can show your bodyweight trend. (The permission sheet also lists reading workouts; the app does not read them today.) All of this happens on your device. Health data is never sent to us or to anyone else, is never used for advertising or marketing, and is not included in analytics, notifications or the suggestion form. You can withdraw access at any time in the Health app under Sharing → Apps → GymLedger, or in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

Purchases

Any optional purchase in GymLedger is processed by Apple through the App Store using StoreKit. GymLedger never sees your payment method, Apple ID, or billing details — Apple processes the payment and we never receive card data. Apple's privacy policy governs that transaction.

To know whether GymLedger Pro is unlocked on your device, the app uses RevenueCat, a purchase-management service. This happens on every launch, whether or not you buy anything. RevenueCat receives a random, anonymous app user identifier, Apple's purchase receipt if there is one, and technical details about the device and app: Apple's identifier for our apps on your device (IDFV, which is not the advertising identifier), device model, iOS version, app version, language and App Store country. It returns your purchase status (which product, whether it is active, and when it renews or expires). It does not receive your name, email, Apple ID or payment details, and it never receives your workouts.

No account required

GymLedger requires no sign-up, no email address, no phone number, and no social login to work. The one exception is optional: the "Suggest a Feature" form asks for an email so we can reply. There is no GymLedger account and no GymLedger server that stores your identity or your training log.

Children

GymLedger is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. The only personal data the app can collect from anyone is an email address, and only if that person chooses to send us feedback.

Data breach risk

Because your workout data is stored only on your device and is never uploaded, there is no GymLedger database of your training log to breach. The records we hold are the feedback messages people chose to send (which include an email address) and the anonymous device records described above. Feedback messages and cross-promotion counts are in a Google Cloud database that only we can read; the device records are on a server we operate ourselves. RevenueCat holds the purchase status records described under Purchases, and Apple and Google hold the push token for delivery. If your device is compromised, that is governed by Apple's device security model — we recommend using a strong passcode and Face ID / Touch ID.

Retention

Your workouts, routines, history, and bodyweight entries live only on your device and are deleted when you delete GymLedger or clear them yourself. The anonymous events described above are retained only for as long as needed to improve the app and prioritise features, and carry nothing that identifies you. Feedback you sent is kept until we no longer need it to answer you or plan the app, or until you ask us to delete it — email us at the address under Contact.
